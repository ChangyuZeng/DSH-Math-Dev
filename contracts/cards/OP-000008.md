## Problem statement

Let $G=\{0,1,\ldots,74\}^2$ be the $75\times75$ integer grid. Construct a subset $P\subseteq G$ containing at least 149 distinct points such that no three points of $P$ lie on one straight line.

## Definitions and assumptions

- Coordinates must be integer pairs $(x,y)$ with $0\leq x,y\leq74$. Points must be distinct.
- Lines in every direction count, including vertical lines and every rational slope, not only rows, columns, and diagonals at $45^\circ$.
- No quarter-turn, reflection, central, or other symmetry restriction is permitted.
- Every row contains at most two selected points, so the elementary upper bound is $|P|\leq150$. The targets of 149 and 150 are distinct: 149 improves the lower bound, whereas 150 attains this upper bound.

## Solution requirements

Submit `points: [[x_1,y_1],...,[x_m,y_m]]` with $m\in\{149,150\}$ and lexicographic ordering. Independently check the count, integer coordinates, distinctness, and boundary conditions. For every unordered triple of distinct points, verify the exact integer determinant

$$(x_2-x_1)(y_3-y_1)-(y_2-y_1)(x_3-x_1)\neq0.$$

All triples must be checked; sampling or checking only selected slopes is insufficient. At the 150-point cap there are $\binom{150}{3}=551300$ triples.

A 149-point witness improves the finite-instance lower bound. A 150-point witness proves that the maximum is 150 for this grid. A 148-point configuration is a known reproduction, and an unsuccessful local exchange or a restriction to a symmetry class does not prove that the unrestricted target is impossible.
