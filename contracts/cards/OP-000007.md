## Problem statement

The seven-dimensional hypercube $Q_7$ has vertex set $\{0,1\}^7$, represented by the integers $0,\ldots,127$. Two vertices are adjacent exactly when their binary representations differ in one coordinate, equivalently when their integer representations $u,v$ satisfy $\operatorname{popcount}(u\oplus v)=1$.

Construct a set $E$ of at least 305 distinct edges of $Q_7$ such that the subgraph $(V(Q_7),E)$ contains no four-cycle $C_4$.

## Definitions and assumptions

- $Q_7$ has 128 vertices and 448 admissible edges. Every selected edge must satisfy $u<v$ and $\operatorname{popcount}(u\oplus v)=1$.
- A square is determined by two distinct coordinate directions $1\leq a<b\leq7$ and an assignment of the other five coordinates. It contains the four edges obtained by toggling $a$ and $b$ independently. There are $\binom72 2^5=672$ such squares, and every $C_4$ in $Q_7$ has this form.
- The construction is unrestricted: no odd-square condition, prescribed degree sequence, automorphism orbit, bipartition distribution, or other symmetry condition may be added.
- The odd-square subclass is the family of edge sets meeting every square in exactly one or three edges. Its exact maximum $g(7)=304$ must not be imposed on this task.

## Solution requirements

Submit `edges: [[u_1,v_1],...,[u_m,v_m]]` with $m\geq305$, distinct endpoints, valid hypercube edges, no repeated edge, and lexicographic ordering. Independently enumerate all 672 squares and verify that each contains at most three selected edges. The witness passes only if every such check succeeds.

A valid witness proves $ex(Q_7,C_4)\geq305$; it does not determine the exact extremal value. A 304-edge construction is a known reproduction, not a new result. An odd-square upper bound, a catalogue of 304-edge examples, solver UNKNOWN, a timeout, or an unsuccessful local search does not prove that no 305-edge construction exists. A proof of the global upper bound 304 would be a different claim and does not satisfy this construction task.
